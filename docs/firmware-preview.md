# Firmware in the preview

The 3D view and the Job timeline draw a plate's program as its machine runs it. The NC says where the tool goes in work coordinates, but for some codes it leaves the moves to the machine's firmware: tool changes, probing routines and moves in machine coordinates. A machine's kit can describe how its firmware moves for them, as a `FirmwareModel` (`src/domain/firmware/firmware-model.ts`). The viewer then draws the plate's machine program (`src/app/workspace/machine-program.ts`): the same NC, parsed with the firmware placing those moves on the plate's bed through its anchors, work origin, stock, fixtures and bed. It is parsed again when the program or that setup changes. Checks, toolpath bounds and the cut prediction keep reading the program as written, as does the G-code list. The same placing takes what the machine reports to the plate's bed (`FirmwareModel.bedPosition`, and `machinePosition` back): the work origin it keeps and, while the Job tab follows a job, where it is ([device-jobs.md](device-jobs.md#the-job-tab)), and on the simulator the tool its camera panel shows and the bed it probes against ([device-controls.md](device-controls.md#simulator)).

Everything the probes do (T0, and the 3D probe's T9999) is drawn green, apart from cutting (the theme's colour) and travel, and so is every probing touch, such as a tool's on the tool setter. Playback shows the probe on the sample it is probing, and the tool where the firmware leaves it after a change; where no tool is known yet, such as before a program's first tool change, none shows. **Play** on the Job tab runs these moves at their feeds ([device-jobs.md](device-jobs.md#the-job-tab)): the firmware's own at the rates it makes them, and the program's as the firmware keeps its rates (`GCodeFirmware.rates`): on the Z1, rapids at the seek rate, 2000 mm/min until an F in `G0` mode sets another, and feed moves at 1000 mm/min before any F.

## The Makera Z1

`src/domain/fixtures/makera-z1/firmware.ts` follows the Z1's firmware 1.1.2 (`resources/MakeraZ1Firmware`: ATCHandler.cpp, CartGridStrategy.cpp, ZProbe.cpp and `configZ1.default`). Where a touch stops, the preview stops at what is below the probe: the tool setter, else the highest top there of the stock, the plate's enabled fixtures and what carries the stock (its bed or wasteboard). A fixture meets the probe where it is solid: the kit's L-brackets, top clamp and 4th axis by the boxes of their parts, any other fixture by its bounding box (`fixtureSolids`, `src/domain/fixtures/solids.ts`). A probe already below the top it would touch halts the machine, and the preview draws nothing further of that block.

After `M6`, `M495`, `M480` and `G28` the firmware restores the distance mode, feed and units; a `G10 L20` offset stays, as does the work origin a 3D probing routine sets, so the rest of the program is drawn where the routine puts it. The preview reads `M480`'s subcode as written, as the firmware reads its digits as a whole number: `M480.10`, the boss's routine, is not `M480.1`. The F of a probing block is its own, not the program's feed. `M495`'s Z probe for the fourth axis (O without F) is not followed, nor are grids without R1; the preview leaves those blocks out, as it does codes it does not know.

### Heights in machine coordinates

Machine X and Y reach the bed through the plate's anchors. Machine Z depends on the tool: the firmware measures each tool on the tool setter when it changes to it, and moves in machine coordinates put the spindle, not the tip, at their Z. The preview takes every tool to meet the setter at machine Z −76.54, and the setter's top to stand at bed Z 6.83 (Z 0 is the MDF bed's top): on a Z1 Pro (Makera Studio's log, 2026-09-22) the wired probe met the setter at −76.54 and, 5.23 mm lower, the stock at anchor 1, taken for a 1.6 mm PCB on the MDF bed. So the clearance is drawn about 79 mm above such a PCB, and a tool that sits lower or higher in its holder is drawn off by the difference. Everything after the first touch is measured from what it touched.

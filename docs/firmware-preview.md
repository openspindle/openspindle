# Firmware in the preview

The 3D view and the Job timeline draw a plate's program as its machine runs it. The NC says where the tool goes in work coordinates, but for some codes it leaves the moves to the machine's firmware: tool changes, probing routines and moves in machine coordinates. A machine's kit can describe how its firmware moves for them, as a `FirmwareModel` (`src/domain/firmware/firmware-model.ts`). The viewer then draws the plate's machine program (`machineProgramOf`, `src/app/job/plan-store.ts`): the same NC, parsed with the firmware placing those moves on the plate's bed through its anchors, work origin, stock, fixtures and bed. It starts where the machine waits once homed (`FirmwareModel.home`; on the Z1 machine X, Y and Z −1, 1 mm back off each home switch), so a tool change before the program's first move returns there rather than over the work origin. It is parsed once per program and setup, and kept for the program's last four setups and for any setup a Run's plan holds. A Run is planned from where the machine is instead: from the tool's tip in the latest fresh status (the machine position less the tool offset), and the Job tab draws that job by its plan's program. Checks, toolpath bounds and the cut prediction keep reading the program as written, as does the G-code list. The same placing takes what the machine reports to the plate's bed (`FirmwareModel.bedPosition`, and `machinePosition` back): the work origin it keeps and, while the Job tab follows a job, where it is ([device-jobs.md](device-jobs.md#the-job-tab)), and on the simulator the tool its camera panel shows and the bed it probes against ([device-controls.md](device-controls.md#simulator)).

Everything the probes do (T0, and the 3D probe's T9999) is drawn green, apart from cutting (the theme's colour) and travel, and so is every probing touch, such as a tool's on the tool setter. Playback shows the probe on the sample it is probing, and the tool making each move: at a change, the old tool on its way to where the change waits, then the new one; where no tool is known yet, such as before a program's first tool change, none shows. **Play** on the Job tab runs these moves as the machine's planner times them ([timing](#timing), [device-jobs.md](device-jobs.md#the-job-tab)): the firmware's own at the rates it makes them, and the program's as the firmware keeps its rates (`GCodeFirmware.rates`). On the Z1, rapids move at the seek rate, and feed moves before any F at the feed rate, as the machine's configuration sets them (2000 and 1000 mm/min by default); an F in `G0` mode sets the seek rate. Where G0 keeps a rate of its own, a block that starts with an F is a `G1` block, as Smoothieware reads it, so its F sets the feed rather than G0's rate. An `M220` in the program scales every move's rate but a probe's search.

## Timing

A plate's moves are timed as its machine's planner runs them, in its motion plan (`MotionPlan`, `src/domain/motion`; [architecture](architecture.md#motion-plans-and-following-a-job)). For the Z1 that is a replica of the Smoothieware planner its firmware runs (`src/domain/motion/planner.ts`, after `Robot.cpp`, `Planner.cpp`, `Block.cpp` and `Conveyor.cpp`):

- A move's speed is its rate capped by each axis's rate along its direction and by the path's maximum speed; its acceleration is the machine's, capped by an axis's own where one is set.
- The speed it turns into the next move at follows the junction deviation (`z_junction_deviation` for a move along Z alone, where set); inside an arc, the junctions of the firmware's own chords cap it. A move starts from rest after the queue empties, after a probe's search and where the path reverses.
- Each move speeds up, cruises and slows down by a trapezoid, its speeds planned backwards and forwards over a full queue of blocks: a line one block for every 5 mm, an arc one for each of the firmware's chords.
- The queue empties before and after each probe search, around each routine (`M6`, `M495`, `M480`, `G28`, `G32`), and at a `G4` that dwells, `M400`, `M3` and `M4`, an `M5` with the spindle on, the program pauses (`M0`, `M1`, `M600`) and the program's end. After it empties, a move the player streams waits up to the queue delay for the queue to fill; a probe's search and a routine's moves start at once. `G10`, `G53` and `M220` do not stop the machine.
- `M3` or `M4` from off dwells the spindle's start delay, and `M5` from on its stop delay; `G4` dwells P seconds (the firmware runs in `grbl_mode`) and S whole seconds more.
- A probe's search ends at its touch without slowing down, as the touch stops the motors.

Moves at the firmware's default rates, which the program never set, run at the machine's own, in the same proportion: the 3D probe's descent at a tenth of the seek rate. The plan's clock counts from the first move at 100 % override, its dwells included; the waits for the user, at a tool change or a pause, take no time in it.

The limits come from the machine's configuration (`/sd/config.txt`) as the Device page last read it on the current connection, for a plate set up for the connected device; otherwise from those the firmware builds in (`Z1_MOTION`, `src/domain/fixtures/makera-z1/motion.ts`). The file is read as the firmware reads it: a line's first word is its key and its next its value, `#` starts a comment, and of duplicate keys the last wins. A plan is built once per program, setup, start and limits; the configuration's revision is part of its key.

| Limit              | Configuration                                                                                                                                                                                      | Built in                   |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Seek rate          | `default_seek_rate`                                                                                                                                                                                | 2000 mm/min                |
| Feed rate          | `default_feed_rate`                                                                                                                                                                                | 1000 mm/min                |
| Axis rates         | The least of the motor's `alpha_`, `beta_` or `gamma_max_rate`, the axis's `x_`, `y_` or `z_axis_max_speed`, and the steps the motor can take (`base_stepping_frequency` over its `_steps_per_mm`) | X and Y 1200, Z 600 mm/min |
| Path speed         | `max_speed`                                                                                                                                                                                        | None                       |
| Acceleration       | `acceleration`; per axis `alpha_`, `beta_` or `gamma_acceleration` (Z also `z_acceleration`)                                                                                                       | 150 mm/s², none per axis   |
| Junction deviation | `junction_deviation`, `z_junction_deviation`                                                                                                                                                       | 0.01 mm, none for Z        |

Until the configuration is read the plan can be well off: a Z1 Pro's protocol trace showed its rapids at 3000 mm/min and its Z rising at about 1000 mm/min, where the built-in limits give 2000 and 600. Following a job learns the pace the machine actually goes at ([device-jobs.md](device-jobs.md#the-job-tab)).

## What the machine reports

The plan also holds what the firmware reports during each move, which following a job compares with the machine's status reports:

- **The line** (`P:`): the line of the last feed move (`G1`, `G2`, `G3`, a `G53 G1` included) of the program's own. Rapids, probe searches and routines keep the line before them, and a pause reports its own line.
- **The tools** (`T:`): the tool the firmware takes to be in the spindle, and the one a change asks for. On the way to a manual change it reports the tool it held and the new one; from the user's confirmation, while it measures the new tool, no tool (−1, `M493.2 T-1`); then the new one. `M495`'s change to the probe does the same. Before the program's first change the tool is one the preview cannot tell, as Run leaves the tool the machine reports as it was.
- **Where it waits** for the user: after the move to where a tool change waits, and at each pause.
- **The position it is placed by**: a routine's moves and a `G53` block's own by the machine position, through the plate's anchors; the program's other moves by the work position, which the work offsets the program sets on the way (`G10 L20`, `M480`, `M495`, `G92`) shift, and in Z only once the program has set work Z.

## The Makera Z1

`src/domain/fixtures/makera-z1/firmware.ts` follows the Z1's firmware 1.1.2 (`resources/MakeraZ1Firmware`: ATCHandler.cpp, CartGridStrategy.cpp, ZProbe.cpp and `configZ1.default`). Where a touch stops, the preview stops at what is below the probe: the tool setter, else the highest top there of the stock, the plate's enabled fixtures and what carries the stock (its bed or wasteboard). A fixture meets the probe where it is solid: the kit's L-brackets, top clamp and 4th axis by the boxes of their parts, any other fixture by its bounding box (`fixtureSolids`, `src/domain/fixtures/solids.ts`). A probe already below the top it would touch halts the machine, and the preview draws nothing further of that block.

After `M6`, `M495`, `M480` and `G28` the firmware restores the distance mode, feed and units; a `G10 L20` offset stays, as does the work origin a 3D probing routine sets, so the rest of the program is drawn where the routine puts it. The preview reads `M480`'s subcode as written, as the firmware reads its digits as a whole number: `M480.10`, the boss's routine, is not `M480.1`. The F of a probing block is its own, not the program's feed. `M495`'s Z probe for the fourth axis (O without F) is not followed, nor are grids without R1; the preview leaves those blocks out, as it does codes it does not know.

### Heights in machine coordinates

Machine X and Y reach the bed through the plate's anchors. Machine Z depends on the tool: the firmware measures each tool on the tool setter when it changes to it, and moves in machine coordinates put the spindle, not the tip, at their Z. The preview takes every tool to meet the setter at machine Z −76.54, and the setter's top to stand at bed Z 6.83 (Z 0 is the MDF bed's top): on a Z1 Pro (Makera Studio's log, 2026-09-22) the wired probe met the setter at −76.54 and, 5.23 mm lower, the stock at anchor 1, taken for a 1.6 mm PCB on the MDF bed. So the clearance is drawn about 79 mm above such a PCB, and a tool that sits lower or higher in its holder is drawn off by the difference. Everything after the first touch is measured from what it touched.

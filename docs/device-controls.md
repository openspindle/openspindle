# Device controls

Device's right column includes **Useful tools** directly below **Spindle**. **Anchor 1 probe** opens anchor calibration; **3D probe routines** opens the illustrated outside/inside corner, boss/pocket centre, height-map and outline picker; **Z probe routines** opens the bounded part-top guide for the wired Z probe, conductive XYZ rod or mechanical 3D touch probe; **Probe diameter** opens diameter calibration. **Inspection** provides shortcuts to stock inspection, feature spacing, surface inspection and repeatability. Opening any shortcut sends no machine commands. Anchor 1 and Z probing offer explicit temporary runs without adding project plates. General probing and inspection prepare operations to review in Prepare and run from Job. Native corner, centre and grid cycles remain simulator-only because they cannot enforce the bounded contact checks; the picker and inspection guide state this restriction.

On Job, **Probe part Z before cutting** opens the Z guide at Run, then requires **Continue to cutting** after a verified surface measurement before the normal cutter-change prompt. See [Z surface](touch-off.md) for setup, limits and restrictions.

## Positioning from a picked point

In Prepare, right-click a shown stock, fixture or bed surface and choose **Jog to point**. The resizable right sidebar shows the picked bed coordinates and target machine coordinates. Opening it sends nothing. The selected tool must be a supported library probe, active and measured with its signal cable connected; cutters are refused. Run **Run tool-setter detection** there, or explicitly confirm the existing measurement of the same fitted probe. Reconnection or uncertain state cannot reuse a completed run.

**X / Y · retract Z first** retracts to machine clearance Z −3 (or keeps an already higher Z), then moves X and Y separately at 100% of their configured axis maximum rates. It leaves Z raised. **Z · 5 mm above surface** leaves X/Y fixed, requires the current position within 0.5 mm of the picked XY, and approaches the model surface plus 5 mm at 20% of the configured Z rate. Its model-to-machine height uses the reported work Z origin and fitted tool offset. You must separately confirm that real work Z0 matches the plate's modeled origin; tool measurement alone does not establish that registration.

Before **Run reviewed move**, verify one touch and release through the automatic signal panel and explicitly confirm the tool measurement, model registration, clear travel, closed enclosure, supervision and accepted risks. Model/anchor edits invalidate the target; changed work Z requires reconfirmation. A conductive rod cannot detect insulating obstacles. The model and 5 mm offset do not prove physical clearance or prevent damage from a failed input or stopping distance.

**Probe part Z here…** has its own combined **Move to point and probe Z** action: measure the fitted probe, verify its signal, confirm the route, retract to machine clearance, move to the picked XY, then probe down from that raised height. It does not need manual XY positioning or a model-height approach. The Z search is capped by the selected maximum and the machine's live lower Z limit. The separate **Z · 5 mm above surface** positioning action retains its model registration and offset requirements.

The Z positioning guide offers **Probe part Z here instead…** when a real surface measurement is intended. Touching stock during positioning remains a failed safety stop and does not set Z0. A modeled 5 mm offset is not a measured physical clearance; tool-setter detection alone does not register the part surface.

Each leg uses a firmware probe-aware transit (`G38.3`): detected contact stops the move, fails the run and prevents the next leg. A no-contact report must match the entire bounded travel and stationary machine endpoint before continuing. The host checks actual enabled, homed XYZ limits with read-only `M211` before every G38 move, including Anchor 1, diameter and Z contacts: firmware probing bypasses ordinary soft limits. Missing, disabled, malformed or exceeded limits stop execution. No searches, transits or withdrawals are retried. Positioning runs share temporary-run progress, tool prompts and Stop without creating project plates; reopening or navigating cannot restart them. These new transits and live limit checks still require physical verification.

One module owns the machine: `MachineController` in `src/machine/core`, hosted in the machine process, an Electron utility process of its own ([architecture.md](architecture.md#the-machine)). The renderer and the native menu reach it only through a `MachineGateway` for their principal, each over its own port. Requests are the closed `MachineCommand` union (Zod-validated at the RPC boundary and again in the controller), plus Run, reads, writing the stored anchors or firmware configuration, a line typed in the [console](#protocol-trace-and-console) and Stop.

## Admission and availability

Connect device accepts a physical Z1 on the local network (discovery or a manually entered IPv4 address, normally port 2222) and the local simulator. Connection verifies the firmware identity and telemetry; connecting does not jog, home, start the spindle or run a program. Ordinary screen controls and Job Run use the existing physical adapter. Gamepad Step mode supports finite physical Z1 jogging; Direct steering uses the local simulator only. Physical gamepad motion and facing still require hands-on verification.

Every request passes one admission chain, the command rules (`contract/command-rules.ts`, [rules](workspace-model.md#rules)) that `core/admission.ts` runs, before anything is sent:

1. a verified connection, and no lockout (an earlier outcome that is unknown);
2. fresh status (at most 5 s old);
3. no other operation in progress (the controller runs one at a time; nothing queues);
4. while a program streams, only light, beep, overrides, pause, resume and tool confirmation are admitted, anchor and height-map reads are **deferred** until the program ends, and configuration transfers, writing the anchors or a typed console line are refused;
5. the capability is reported by the machine (for example, no tool confirmation on ATC machines);
6. the firmware's machine-state rules (`firmware/makera/commands.ts`).

The same chain produces `snapshot.availability`, so every disabled control shows the exact reason the controller would refuse it. Stop needs a connection and something to stop (`machine/stop-needed`): a lockout to confirm, an operation in progress, a program, or a machine not freshly reporting Idle. On the Z1 Stop halts the controller into an alarm, so on an idle machine it would only leave it to unlock. Machine › Stop follows the same availability. Reset needs a connection, fresh status, no lockout and no running program or operation. A typed console line goes in any machine state: the firmware answers what it will not run.

Reads are shared by kind (anchors, height map). A read asked for while one of its kind runs joins it instead of being refused as busy, and one the chain defers joins the read of its kind that waits already, so the deferred list holds at most one per kind. One read answers every caller; a caller that cancels withdraws only itself, and a deferred read that every caller left is dropped.

## Verification

A written command is not a successful command. Without a program stream, success needs the firmware acknowledgement **and** a telemetry post-condition. While a program streams, acknowledgements may belong to its played lines, so they are never trusted and only telemetry counts.

| Jog                     | `$J X1 F0.1` (one axis; converted for inch mode)  | Idle at start + distance (±0.005 mm)                                                                                     |
| Home all                | `$H`                                              | `ok`, then Idle (Alarm before the acknowledgement is expected)                                                           |
| Unlock (Alarm only)     | `$X`                                              | `[Caution: Unlocked]` and `ok`, then status leaves Alarm. Homing is advised afterwards.                                  |

| Go to                   | `M496.1`–`M496.4`: clearance, origin, anchor 1, 2 | up to the clearance, then X and Y: Idle after moving, or still Idle 1.5 s on; work X0 Y0 also by position                |

A rejection line fails the command. An unverified command reports that its outcome is unknown and never retries; unverified motion (jog, home, spindle) locks out further commands until Stop is confirmed or the device is connected again. The open TCP connection continues listening and keeps Stop available; fresh status alone does not clear the lockout. A late acknowledgement of an unverified command is swallowed for two seconds.

The Device tab's **Accessories** card stores separate work-light brightness percentages for the app's Light and Dark appearance, labelled **Daylight brightness** and **Night brightness**. Both start at 100%; System appearance follows the operating system's current light or dark mode. Tooltips explain the theme mapping and the inactivity timer. These preferences belong to the app and persist across launches. Turning the light on applies the active percentage directly. A theme or brightness change while the light is on applies once the device is Idle with no active program or other operation; changes made while busy use the latest preference when it becomes available, even on another tab. A light that is off stays off until turned on explicitly. The first automatic brightness update on each connection also waits for the firmware command channel: the read-only `model` query must itself report Idle, followed by fresh diagnostics and status. Startup can report Idle before automatic homing begins, so status alone is insufficient. The app polls only that read-only query, for up to 30 seconds; it sends brightness once readiness is confirmed. Each brightness command is tied to the connection that requested it and rechecks admission before sending; failed commands are reported without automatic retries, except one refused because another operation started first (such as the configuration read on connecting), which is sent again once that operation has ended. Dimming requires the firmware's work-light switch to support PWM with a maximum value of 255.

The **Work light** fieldset groups the on/off switch, both brightness preferences, the inactivity timer and, when it is on, the machine's own light timer into horizontal fields. The timer accepts whole minutes from 0 to 1440; **0 means Never** and is the default. It belongs to OpenSpindle and works while the app is connected, including on other tabs. It counts continuous machine idle time with no active program and a stopped spindle. Machine activity, turning the light off, reconnecting, changing the timer or a gap in fresh status starts a new countdown. Once the timer expires, the app waits for other operations to finish, confirms the firmware is ready and checks fresh status before sending one verified light-off command to the same connection. Changing the timer or losing the idle condition cancels a request still waiting to send; a sent command always finishes verification. Failed commands are reported without automatic retries, except one refused because another operation started first, which waits for it like the timer does. The light stays off until turned on explicitly. This preference applies immediately and does not edit the firmware configuration or restart the machine.

**Machine light timer** shows only while the firmware's own light timer would darken a dimmed light, as the configuration the Device page has read says: `light.turn_off_min` above 0, with a PWM work light (`switch.light.output_type pwm`) that starts on. While that timer is on, the firmware sets the light to the brightness it stores whenever the machine is not Idle, and a PWM light stores none from `M821 S`, so a dimmed light goes dark as a job, homing or a jog starts. **Turn off** saves `light.turn_off_min 0`, changing only that line and verifying the saved file, under the same conditions as Vacuum power; a file with the key on more than one line is refused until the duplicate is removed in the firmware configuration. It takes effect once the machine restarts ([below](#restart-after-saving)). The inactivity timer can turn the light off instead.

The **Accessories** card starts with **Beep** and **Follow spindle**, followed by the **Work light** and **Vacuum** fieldsets and, for a machine with a camera, **Camera**. The Vacuum fieldset groups its on/off switch and **Vacuum power** control in horizontal fields. Vacuum power reads the saved default from the device configuration and accepts whole percentages from 50 to 100. **Save** updates only `switch.vacuum.default_on_value`, preserving the rest of the configuration and verifying the saved file. Saving requires an idle machine with no active program and a confirmed stopped spindle; editing alone sends nothing. The value belongs to the connection and configuration revision from which it was read. A successful save through the firmware configuration dialog refreshes an untouched Vacuum power field; a draft is preserved. If the file changes underneath a draft, reload it before saving. Saving leaves the current output unchanged until the machine restarts ([below](#restart-after-saving)). After restart, **Follow spindle** uses the saved default when the spindle starts. The manual Vacuum switch turns the output on at 100% or off.

The Camera fieldset's **Video size** sets the camera stream's frame size (`*mainboard.video_stream_framesize`, [Camera](#camera)) to 640 × 480, 800 × 600, 1024 × 768, 1280 × 720, 1280 × 1024 or 1600 × 1200, saved as soon as one is chosen, under the same conditions as Vacuum power, to the configuration the Device page has read. A size the configuration sets that is not among them is shown with them.

<a id="restart-after-saving"></a>The firmware reads its configuration when it starts and keeps nothing of the file after that (`Kernel.cpp` loads it, `main.cpp` clears it once the modules have read theirs), so a saved setting takes effect only after a restart. Once a save over the current connection changed what the machine applies at startup (the configuration, Vacuum power, Video size, the machine light timer or the machine's own anchors; not the places users add), `snapshot.connection.restartNeeded` is set and the Device page shows **Saved settings take effect once the machine restarts** at its top, with **Reset now**, which asks as **Reset** does. A new connection, as after a reset, starts without it; the app cannot tell whether a machine it connects to again was restarted in between.

**Reset** (Device, after a confirmation) reserves the controller before sending the console command `reset`, which reboots the controller three seconds later ("Rebooting machine in 3 seconds..."). Other operations remain unavailable while its frame leaves the connection; Stop can still interrupt it. The connection does not outlive a reboot, so the session ends after the frame has had 300 ms to leave and the controller connects to the same device again: first after five seconds, then every three, for up to 90 seconds. Meanwhile `snapshot.connection.restarting` is set and failed attempts are not reported as errors; connecting or disconnecting by hand ends the wait.

Stop preempts everything: it cancels an in-flight transfer (the B5 frame, see [Transaction](device-jobs.md#transaction)), aborts the current operation and every deferred read, then sends the halt and waits for Alarm. Replies from canceled commands and transfers drain for two seconds; they cannot acknowledge a later operation, and Stop's telemetry confirmation does not wait for them. A shorter keyed-reply cleanup does not shorten that cancellation drain. Machine › Stop (⌘.) goes from the main process straight to the controller in the machine process, over the main process's own port, so it works even if the renderer is unresponsive; and the controller's loop does nothing but the machine's work, so nothing else delays it. An unconfirmed Stop sets a lockout; only Stop is admitted until the machine is reconnected or confirms a Stop, and the Device page and the Job tab show it as a finding with **Stop** ([diagnosis](#diagnosis)). That holds for Reset too, whose reconnection would otherwise clear the lockout before anyone checked the machine. Stop does not replace the physical emergency stop.

## Gamepad controls

The Jog card offers Xbox-style gamepad controls with standard Gamepad API mapping. Other mappings remain input-monitor only. Arming is explicit and requires centred sticks and released buttons. Step mode sends one finite X, Y or Z jog per gesture, with LB held; Direct steering is available only on the simulator. RB with D-pad left/right selects step size; L3 cycles jog speed. B requests Stop and disarms. X is the spindle modifier, with A to start, Y to stop and D-pad up/down to change target RPM.

Controller ownership is tied to the current machine connection. Focus loss, stale input, controller changes and mode changes require rearming. Physical interrupted motion uses the firmware halt into Alarm; an unconfirmed stop keeps the connection locked out with Stop available. See [gamepad-prototype.md](gamepad-prototype.md) for the bindings and physical-verification limits.

# Workspace model

The workspace is what the user works on: plates, the tool and stock libraries, measured height maps, the project's rule settings and its name. It is plain immutable data (`WorkspaceState`, `src/domain/workspace/workspace.ts`) changed only by commands. Code: `src/domain/**`.

## Plates

A plate is one setup on the machine bed and the program machined from it. Its `name` is empty until the user gives it one; until then it shows as "Plate N", numbered by its place in the workspace (`plateLabel`). The plate that replaces a new project's empty plate takes the name given to that one (`plates.add`). Importing programs into a plate without a name names it after the first program (`programName`, `src/app/workspace/import-plan.ts`): its Fusion NC program's name, else its file's name without the extension.

- **Setup** (`PlateSetup`): the stock and where its minimum corner sits on the bed (`stockAnchor`; its X and Y can be kept relative to a stored anchor, `stockRelativeTo`, like the work origin's), **one work origin** (`workOrigin`, the program's NC zero, in bed coordinates: millimetres from the machine's first anchor, Anchor 1 on the Z1, with Z 0 on the MDF bed's top, [stored-anchors.md](stored-anchors.md#coordinate-registration); its X and Y can be kept relative to a stored anchor, `workOriginAnchor`, which it follows when the anchors change; a plate with a [touch-off](touch-off.md) keeps its Z on the stock top, where the touch sets work Z0: `applyCommand` holds every plate a command brings in or changes to that, `withTouchedWorkOrigin`), **one assist policy** (vacuum, air, bed cleaning, anti-static on Run), the fixtures (each a snapshot of its definition, whose model is bundled with the app, a box or in the [Models library](models.md), and its placement, which a lock keeps and whose X and Y can be kept relative to a stored anchor, `relativeTo`; a wasteboard is one), the device the plate is set up for and its [bed setup](stored-anchors.md#bed-setups) (`bedSetupId`), and a snapshot of that device's anchors with the bed setup's. Every operation machines from the plate's work origin. Stock chosen for a plate without stock keeps the corner the plate had (Anchor 1 for a new plate), resting on what carries it there, with the work origin on its top front-left corner; a work origin on the stock's top stays on it when the stock's height changes, by another stock or its **Height** (`withStockChange`).
- **Setup items** (`src/domain/plate/setup-items.ts`): what the 3D view selects and moves: the machine's bed (a bed fixture lies on it and covers its holes), the enabled fixtures with a model (wasteboards among them), the stock and the design (the program at its work origin), each with its mount points in bed coordinates. `plate.moveItem` moves a fixture by its position, the stock with the work origin, or the work origin alone; beds and locked fixtures stay in place. Prepare's 3D view also picks a probing operation's start on them ([probing.md](probing.md)), and leaves out a fixture hidden in the Plates list ([below](#the-plates-list)).
- **Fixtures** (`src/domain/plate/plate-fixtures.ts`): the fixture commands hold a plate's fixtures to their rules; `plate.setup` does not change them. A plate has one bed: `fixture.setBed`, and `fixture.add` with a bed, keep the plate's own fixture of that bed's definition as it is (enabled), or else add the new one, and every other bed goes. A locked fixture (`isLocked`; beds stay in place without a lock) refuses `fixture.update` (a move, a turn, another origin or anchor) and `fixture.remove` until `fixture.lock` unlocks it. A plate holds at most 32 fixtures (`FIXTURE_LIMIT`, which also bounds a bed setup's definitions). `fixtures.useDefaults` sets the plate up on one of its device's [bed setups](stored-anchors.md#bed-setups) (`bedSetupId`): its fixtures and anchors, keeping the plate's locked fixtures. `fixtures.redefine`, which editing a fixture's definition on the Device page dispatches ([models.md](models.md#fixtures)), gives the plates set up on that bed setup that have the fixture the definition as it now is, where they have it; when that bed setup is the device's default, so do the plates of the device set up on no bed setup.
- **Tool table** (`tools`): the plate owns the T numbers. Each entry maps a number to a library tool (`toolId`), or to none yet. Entry `null` is the implicit tool of a program that selects none. T0 is always the probe, and T9999 the 3D probe, as the Z1 firmware numbers them.
- **Operations**: the programs the plate runs, in order.
- **Groups**: named selections of program sections, by stable section id.
- **Notices**: what an import changed, until the user dismisses them.

A new project starts with an empty plate 1: the selected device profile's default bed setup's bed, fixtures and anchors, without stock or operations, its stock corner and work origin at the bed's origin, Anchor 1 on the Z1 (`defaultPlateCoordinates`; a plate with stock has it centred in the work area). It is a placeholder (`example`): the first plate of the user's own replaces it, and plates that replace the empty one keep its fixtures, device and anchors (`useImportContext`). While it is still empty, without stock or operations, connecting a device sets it up on that device's default bed setup, with its device, anchors and fixtures, without the project counting as changed (`followDeviceBed`, `src/app/workspace/project-session.ts`): a program imported then looks for its fixtures in that bed setup, as fixtures are a device's ([Fusion 360](fusion360.md#stock-and-fixtures)). A plate added with + in the Plates header replaces nothing (`useAddPlate`): it has the default stock on the selected profile, and goes after the empty plate, which stays as a plate of the user's own (`plate.keep`). Once stock is set up on the empty plate, the plates that replace it keep its whole setup (`keptSetup`): the stock and where it sits, and the work origin. That holds for generated and PCB operations and for plain NC programs, over the stock their markers describe; exported plates keep their own setup. Otherwise a plain NC program starts a plate with the stock its markers describe: OpenSpindle's stock markers place it from a stored anchor and put the work origin on it, both kept relative to that anchor so Run sets the machine's work X and Y from it (`markedStock` and `withStockPlacement`), and give it the device's fixtures they name where they put them, keeping the program's heights between the stock and the fixtures under it (`withProgramFixtures`, [Fusion 360](fusion360.md#stock-and-fixtures)), and Makera CAM's give its size. A probing operation machines nothing and brings no stock: added over the empty plate, it keeps that plate's setup as it is, and a new plate it starts has none (`targetPlate` in `src/app/workspace/defaults.ts`).

### The Plates list

Prepare's Plates list (`src/features/prepare/plate-tree/`) shows each plate with two levels under it, **Fixtures** and **Operations**, expanded until collapsed; its search finds plates, fixtures, operations and sections. A plate's row selects it, moves it up or down and removes it.

- **Fixtures** lists the fixtures on the plate's bed (its enabled ones), named as its **Fixtures** panel names them, with how many; clicking it shows that panel. A fixture's row selects it as clicking it in the 3D view does, and hides it in the 3D view (the eye; a fixture without a model is not drawn and has none), locks it (all but a bed) and removes it (a locked one is refused until it is unlocked), from the row or its context menu. **+** on **Fixtures**, or **Add fixture…** in its context menu or a fixture's, opens **Add fixture**: the fixtures of the plate's bed setup, grouped by kind (beds, wasteboards, clamps, holders, vises, vacuum beds, rotary modules, other), each with a picture rendered from its model, isometric and in its colour and material, as the Device page's fixtures show it (its kind's icon without one), its size (width × depth × height, mm) and **On plate** where the plate has it already (**2 on plate** for two). Choosing one adds it as the panel's **Add fixture…** does, and shows the panel: the fixture added is selected, to be moved into place, and a bed replaces the plate's bed (one the plate has already stays, selected).
- **Operations** lists the plate's operations in the order they run, with how many; its **+**, or **Add operation…** in its context menu, adds one to the plate.

The eye on **Fixtures** and on **Operations** hides all of them in the 3D view, or shows them again once all are hidden, as **Hide all** and **Show all** in their context menus do. Hiding is a setting of the view, never saved and not an edit: a hidden operation's toolpath is not drawn, and a hidden fixture is not drawn, picked or snapped to, nor does a probing start snap to its corners, holes or centres.

## Operations

An operation has a name, a revision (incremented on every change, so an editor saving against an older revision is refused), a Pause before switch, its tool bindings and a source:

| Source        | NC                                                                                                                                                                        | Phase                                                                      |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `file`        | the imported NC, byte for byte unless importing split or fixed it; without its closing park when `park` is off; one from Fusion 360 keeps its `origin` to be updated from | machining; a saved phase is retained for NC migrated from an older project |
| `pcb`         | generated by pcb2gcode from its saved Gerber or Excellon source and recipe; null until generated, which blocks Run and export                                             | machining                                                                  |
| `unsupported` | an older source without generated NC, kept intact; blocks Run and export until replaced or removed                                                                        | its saved phase                                                            |
| `probing`     | written by its strategy's method when compiling, from its parameters, the plate and its probe, so it can never go stale (an outline from the plate's toolpath bounds)     | setup                                                                      |

Each source kind is registered in `src/domain/operations/kinds.ts` with how it resolves to NC (and what that NC may contain: a probing operation's task, which is a probed grid, a touch-off, an outline traced with the probe's pointer or a corner or centre found with a 3D probe; no probing otherwise), its phase and whether a lone operation may be emitted byte for byte. What an operation is checked against while editing and before Run are operation and run [rules](#rules). Kinds resolve, and the operation rules check, with the kit of the plate's machine (`kitForPlate`, `src/domain/fixtures/catalog.ts`: the kit of the plate's device, else the one its fixtures come from, else the default kit a new workspace starts from) and the tool library (`ResolveContext`).

A probing operation ([probing.md](probing.md)) is `{ kind: "probing", task, strategy, probe, params }`. Its `strategy` is what it is to do, by id (`PROBING_STRATEGIES`, `src/domain/probing/strategies.ts`): `outside-corner`, `inside-corner`, `pocket-center` or `boss-center` (task `origin`, whose `routine` parameter is always its strategy's), `touch-off` (Z surface), `height-map` (task `grid`) or `outline-trace` (task `outline`). Its `task` decides the shape of `params`. Which method writes its NC is the plate's machine's to decide (`methodFor`): of the methods that perform the strategy on it (its firmware's cycles, then OpenSpindle's own NC where the machine gives what that is made of), the first that is not blocked on the plate with the operation's parameters, else the first, whose NC then says why it cannot (`probing-invalid`). A strategy the machine has no method for, an id OpenSpindle does not know, or a strategy of another task does not resolve (`probing-unsupported`). Its `probe` is the T number its NC selects the probe by, which its tool binding maps into the plate's table like any tool number. Resolving takes the library tool that entry holds (`boundProbe`, `src/domain/probing/bound-probe.ts`): it must be a probe of known profile that the method runs with on the machine, in the number the machine's firmware needs a probe of that profile in (on the Z1, T0 for a Z touch probe and T9999 for a 3D probe); otherwise the operation does not resolve (`probing-probe`), naming the entry and the tool. The ranges and defaults of its parameters are the method's on the machine (`ProbingMethod.parameters`). Values along X and Y are pairs, X then Y: a grid's `size` and `points`, 3D probing's `distance`. 3D probing takes its ball from the probe's diameter. **Probing** lists the strategies and adds an operation for the one chosen, named after it, with the defaults of the method that would run it fitted to the plate, and a library probe that can perform it bound into the plate's table (`newProbingOperation`); the inspector's **Strategy** changes it to another strategy of its task, keeping its other parameters (`withStrategy`; an operation still named after its strategy takes the new one's name), and **Probe** changes the probe. A grid, a touch-off and 3D probing place their start as setup items keep their points: at the probe position, or at a stored anchor plus an X and Y `offset`, with an optional `height` on the bed apart from it, which 3D probing comes down to first (`ProbePlacement`, `src/domain/probing/placement.ts`). Their forms edit it with the fields the work origin, the stock and fixtures use (`ReferencePointFields`, `src/components/workspace/reference-point-fields.tsx`).

## Tools

`bindTools` binds an operation's own tool numbers (the T words its NC selects) into the plate's table:

- an existing binding stays;
- the probe slots keep their number: T0 is always the probe, T9999 the 3D probe;
- a library tool already in the table is reused, so the machine never changes to the same tool twice;
- otherwise the operation's own number is taken if free, then the tool's post-processor number, then the lowest free number.

An imported NC program prefers, for each of its tool numbers, the library tool it describes (Makera CAM's tool markers, else Fusion-style tool comments; `describedPreferences`, which rules tools out by diameter and kind and ranks them by name and flute length), else the tool with that post-processor number, which Fusion 360 imports skip.

`tool.assign` puts a library tool in an entry (merging into the entry that already holds it); `tool.renumber` moves an entry to another free number and rewrites every binding, refusing to move one to or from a probe slot. Deleting a tool from the library changes no plate: the entry keeps pointing at it, a diagnostic blocks Run and offers to assign another tool. The probe slots must hold a probe and no other entry may (`isProbe` in `src/domain/tools/tool.ts`, which compares a tool's kind as loosely as `toolKindKey` does, so "Probe" and "probe" are the same kind everywhere); either mismatch is its own diagnostic with the same fix.

A tool's `shaft` holds segments (a height and the diameters at both ends) that stack from the shoulder up, such as the taper from the flutes to the shank; Fusion 360 imports read them from the record's Shaft tab. A tool's photo (`image`) is a picture the app bundles or one chosen from a file, scaled to 480 pixels and kept in the tool as a WebP data URL. Its 3D model (`model`) is likewise a binary glTF the app bundles (`/models/….glb`) or one chosen from a file and kept as a data URL (at most 1 MB; `validateGlb` checks it is one self-contained GLB), in glTF's metres, Y up, with the tip at the origin. A probe's profile (`probe`, `probeProfile`) says what it senses, touches along Z only (`touch: "z"`) or in X, Y and Z (`"xyz"`, a 3D touch probe), and whether it carries a laser `pointer`, which traces without touching: the probing a probe can do follows from it, and its ball is its diameter. Only a probe has one, and a probe may have none, of unknown profile, until the tool library says (the tool editor's **Touches** and **Laser pointer**).

Tool records are version 5. Records of versions 2 to 4 are upgraded when read (`upgradeTool` in `src/formats/tool-library/upgrade.ts`), a version at a time: version 4 records gain a probe profile (Makera's bundled probes their catalog's: the Wired Probe 2.0 touches along Z with a pointer, the 3D Probe in X, Y and Z; any other probe numbered T9999 for post-processing a 3D probe's, the rest along Z without a pointer; other tools none), version 3 records gain an empty 3D model, and in version 2 records the fields version 3 added (the shaft, photo, vendor description and grade; the maximum diameter, upper radius, tapered and thread tips and through-tool coolant; the holder's product link; the presets' description, cutting speed, ramp angle, transition and retract feeds and feed-per-revolution mode) come from the tool's Fusion record as an import reads it, keyed as a new tool's, so a saved catalog tool still equals its catalog record. An imported tool keeps that record (`source.raw`) as provenance only: nothing but this upgrade reads it.

`toolShape` (`src/domain/tools/tool-shape.ts`) turns a tool's dimensions into solids of revolution, in millimetres above its tip: the flutes, which cut (a probe's ball, which touches), and the shaft (a neck up to the shoulder length, the shaft's segments, then the shank up to the overall length), which must not touch the work. Each tool type has its cutting end (flat or corner-rounded, ball, drill point, chamfer or V cone, taper, round-over, thread teeth, or the ball on a lollipop mill's neck or a probe's stylus); a dimension the record leaves out falls back to one it gives (a shank as wide as the cutter, flutes one diameter long, a nominal length when the tool's is unknown). The tool's holder is not part of the shape. The tool library draws these shapes, each once, with three.js (`src/features/tool-library/tool-pictures.ts`), and keeps the pictures (`src/app/tools/tool-picture-cache.ts`). Tool cards show a tool's kept thumbnail and need no renderer: the library draws one a card asks for, and every library tool's in the background. During playback, and while a job is followed, the 3D view shows the tool that makes the move on show (`PlaybackFrame.tool`, drawn as the plate's tool of that number, `toolRuns` in `src/features/viewer/viewer-plate.ts`) at the end of the revealed path, or, while **Play** runs the moves or a job is followed, where it is along the move under way, and the probe where it probes: at a grid's active sample and a touch-off's point, since its NC reaches them with moves in machine coordinates. A tool with a 3D model is drawn from the model instead, in its own materials, once it has loaded (the 3D view loads each once per viewer, `ViewerAssets.toolModel`); its dimensions still give its shape.

## Compiling

`compilePlate(plate, tools)` is pure, never throws and is cached per plate object. It reads the tool library (`tools`) only through the plate's table, so it keeps a plate's result while the tools the table's entries hold stay the same: editing a tool no entry holds compiles nothing again, and editing one an entry holds, such as an operation's probe, compiles its plate again. It compiles for the plate's machine: what is its own, such as its clearance, its work offsets, its probing blocks and its CAM's markers, comes from the plate's kit (`kitForPlate`). It returns the program, a span per operation, program sections, pause points and diagnostics. A plate whose operations did not change keeps its program object and sections while its NC text and name stay the same, so moving the stock or the work origin parses nothing again and the 3D view keeps the uploaded path. A program is at most 1,000,000 lines (`MAX_PROGRAM_LINES`), well above what 10 MiB of NC holds.

- **Verbatim**: a plate with a single operation of a verbatim kind, identity tool bindings and no Pause before emits that NC byte for byte.
- **Work origin**: a work origin kept relative to a stored anchor other than the first, or at the bed origin (in bed coordinates, from Anchor 1) once the plate's anchors were read from its device (`workOriginOnMachine`), starts the program with the kit's work offset NC (`workOffsetNc`) at the anchor's machine position plus the offsets, so its plate is always composed; its Run check needs anchors read from the connected device. On the Z1 that is `; Work origin: …`, `G21 G54` and `G10 L2 P0 X… Y…`: `G21 G54` comes first because the firmware reads G10's X and Y in the current units and `P0` is the current work coordinate system, whatever the program before left.
- **Compose**: otherwise each operation is emitted after a header (`; Operation: name`, `G21 G90 G17 G94`, the machine's clearance retract, `M5 M9`, and `M0` when it pauses before), with T words rewritten through its bindings and program ends removed; the program ends with the same modes, retract and `M5 M9`, then `M2`, so no job ends with the tool in the cut. The retract is the machine kit's `clearanceRetract` (`G53 G0 Z-3` on the Z1, the clearance Makera configures): a tool the operation before left in the cut rises out of it still turning, then the spindle stops, and the next operation's first move cannot drag it. It follows the modes, as the firmware reads a `G53` move's Z in the current units. An operation's NC may set only the modal state that header restores (units, distance mode, plane, feed mode), plus the blocks the kit reads as its own (`readNcBlock`): its probing, only in a probing operation of the task it does, and on the Z1 path control (`G61`, `G61.1`, `G64` with optional `P`/`Q`, alone in its block), as the Z1 firmware has no path control and corners by junction deviation (`M205`), so it changes nothing that could carry into the next operation, and `M400`, the extractor port (`M851` with an optional `S`, `M852`), the light (`M821`, `M822`) and the beeper (`M861`, `M862`), each alone in its block. It may end with the machine's park after its last move, a block the kit tells (`isPark`) and reads as its own: on the Z1 `G28` alone in its block, as Makera's CAM ends its programs, for which the firmware lifts Z to its clearance height, then moves X and Y to its clearance position. Anything that moves after a park is refused, and on the Z1 so is `G28` with other words. An NC file's **Park after machining** (`park`) keeps its closing park or leaves those lines empty, in either mode.
- **Sections**: tool changes, toolpaths, probing and other blocks, with ids stable across edits of other operations and tool renumbering (`operationId/kind:name#n`; a tool change is keyed by the operation's own T number). The kit's probing tells its grids and touch-offs (`MachineProbing.sections`), and the markers of its CAM start and name toolpaths (Makera CAM's `;@MKR|TOOLPATH` on the Z1, `src/domain/fixtures/makera-z1/makera-cam.ts`); without them, CAM headings in comments do, as does a comment on a line of its own after a blank line or before a tool change, where Fusion 360's posts name their operations. The G-code list heads them and the Job tab's stages read them; the job's position and the timeline's markers name them with their operation, "Isolation · Toolpath 1" (`positionLabel`). The timeline also marks the pauses compiling adds before operations, and is built once per compiled program (`buildPreviewTimeline`). A group keeps the sections that still exist; one whose sections are all gone is not shown.
- **Lines that cannot run**: every reader takes a line's words from one lexer (`readNcBlock`, `src/machine/contract/nc-block.ts`), which Run's dialect uses too: comments in parentheses or after `;`, words of a letter and a number, M117's text as data, even after a line number, and `%`. A line with an unclosed or unmatched comment, a transport checksum, a block-delete `/`, text that is not NC words or a number out of range cannot run as written: the preview does not draw it and Run refuses the program. Combining refuses its operation (`operation-unsafe`); a program emitted byte for byte reports its first such line as an error (`nc-unreadable`).
- **Toolpath bounds**: `plateToolpathBounds` (`src/domain/compile/toolpath-bounds.ts`) is where a plate cuts: the feed moves that are not the probes', of its operations outside the setup phase, each measured from its own NC. The 3D view outlines it and snaps to it; Fit grid, Center and Outline trace use it ([outline-trace.md](outline-trace.md#toolpath-bounds)).
- **Pause points**: Pause before, a height map's review and the NC's own M0/M1. The Makera dialect sends program stops as `M600` at transfer time.
- A broken operation reports a diagnostic and does not stop the others from compiling.

## Diagnostics

Everything that blocks or qualifies Run is a `Diagnostic` (`src/domain/diagnostics.ts`): `error` or `warning`, a code, a message, what it is about (its `Subject`: the plate, `PlateSubject`; its setup's work origin, `PlateSetupSubject`; an operation, `OperationSubject`; or an entry of the tool table, `ToolSubject`), where it is when it has a place on the bed (`Place`: a `Point`, `Path` or `Area`, in bed coordinates) or lines in the compiled program, and optionally a quick fix. Diagnostics cover compile problems, PCB operations awaiting a toolpath (`operation-pending`), unsupported sources without generated NC (`operation-unsupported`), probing operations without a probe that can perform their strategy (`probing-probe`), and what the tool and operation [rules](#rules) find: unassigned or missing tools, a probe assigned outside a probe slot (T0, and T9999 for the 3D probe) or another tool assigned to one, the placement and stock checks of grids, touch-offs and 3D probing, an outline's extent and order, and a machining operation whose cuts in X and Y all run below the stock or all above it (its program's Z zero is not where the work origin puts it: with the work origin on the stock bottom, a program cutting down from Z0 on the stock top would cut through the stock; `src/domain/compile/stock-depth.ts`). A rule's failure is a diagnostic coded by the rule's id, with the rule's problem as its message, about what the rule names, and with the first fix it offers (`failureDiagnostic`, `src/domain/rules/diagnostics.ts`). `plateDiagnostics` (`src/app/workspace/diagnostics.ts`) gathers them for the Prepare inspector and the Job tab's run checklist, and keeps them per plate object while the tools stay the same; the run rules add what depends on the connected machine (for example that an anchored height grid, or a work origin kept relative to an anchor, uses anchors read from it).

`keyDiagnostics` keys a plate's diagnostics by code and subject, so a problem keeps its key through edits that change its message. The Prepare 3D view marks every problem with a place where it is (`ProblemView` draws a ring at a point, a line along a path and an area's outline, in the severity's colour; a marker over it names the problem on hover): a touch-off's point off the stock, an anchored height grid, an outline beyond the stock, and a machining operation's cuts below or above the stock. Picking a marker selects what the problem is about and shows it (`src/features/viewer/problem-focus.ts`): the view draws it stronger, highlights its program lines while its operation stays selected, and pans to it when it is near an edge or out of view. **Show** in the inspector and in the design rule results shows a problem the same way.

## Rules

What requests to the machine, plates and height maps are checked against are rules (`Rule`, `src/machine/contract/rules.ts`) in one ordered list (`RULES`, `src/domain/rules/rules.ts`): `rulesOf(stage)` gives a stage's rules in list order, `ruleById` a rule by its id. A rule has a unique id, its stage, a label and a description, the severity a failure is reported at unless a project sets another, whether a project sets it (`configurable`) and, for a limit, its unit, range and default. It holds for every machine, or only for those it names by fixture kit id (`machines`; `FixtureKit.id`, `makera-z1` for the Z1). It may be one of a chain of gates (`chain`): a subject that fails one is not tested against the chain's later rules, which assume it passed. Its `test` says only whether a subject keeps it, at the limit it is tested at; for a subject that breaks it, `locate` gives the program lines and `measure` how far, `explain` words the failure (its problem, advice where no fix resolves it, and what its worst subject is) and `fixes` offers the changes that resolve it (`offer`) and makes them (`apply`).

Each stage tests one kind of subject (`src/domain/rules/stages.ts`):

| Stage        | Subject                                                                                                       | Its rules                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `command`    | A request to the machine, with the machine's state as admission reads it                                      | Admission's chain (`machine/…`, `src/machine/contract/command-rules.ts`)                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `run`        | The plate to run, then each of its operations, against the connected machine                                  | A plate with operations (`run/plate`, `run/operations`); anchors read from the connected machine for a work origin kept relative to one (`work-origin/anchors-…`) and for anchored probing (`probing/anchors-…`)                                              |
| `tool`       | An entry of the plate's tool table, with the library tool it names                                            | An assigned tool the library has, and a probe in the probe slots and nowhere else (`tool-…`)                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `operation`  | An operation of a plate, with its machine's kit and the compiled program                                      | What the probing operations need of their anchors, the stock and their order, by task (`grid/…`, `touch-off/…`, `origin/…`, `outline/…`), cuts that do not all run below the stock or all above it (`stock-depth/…`)                                          |
| `move`       | A move of the compiled program that reaches below the stock top                                               | The move rules ([design rules](design-rules.md#move-rules))                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `program`    | NC as written, with what runs before it: an operation's own NC, a program to import, a console line           | The program rules ([design rules](design-rules.md#program-rules))                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `height-map` | A height map read after probing                                                                               | What the review needs to rely on the map and call it flat (`height-map/…`)                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `diagnosis`  | What is known about the connected machine: its fresh status, a lockout, its kit and its switches as last read | An unconfirmed Stop and the E-stop (`diagnosis/lockout`, `diagnosis/estop`), the kit's own findings (the Z1's `diagnosis/z1-…`) and the alarm by name (`diagnosis/alarm`); why it is halted is one chain ([device-controls.md](device-controls.md#diagnosis)) |

One runner, `runRules(rules, subjects, { settings, level, machine, group })`, tests subjects against the rules a run takes: those that hold for `machine`, reported as `settings` say (a rule set to Ignore is not tested) and at `level` or above. It returns the failures in the order subjects first fail them, one per rule and group (without `group`, per subject), each with the severity and limit it was tested at, its first and worst subjects, how many lines break it and which, consecutive ones merged. `rulesSchema` makes rules a Standard Schema of a form's input, each failure an issue with the rule's problem as its message.

What a failure becomes is each view's own:

- **Admission** runs the command rules on every request to the machine, in the machine process: none failing allows it, an error refuses it and a warning defers it, with the rule's problem as the reason ([device-controls.md](device-controls.md)). They give `snapshot.availability` too; the renderer never runs them.
- **The run checklist** (`RUN_CHECKLIST`, `src/features/job/run-checklist.ts`) is rows over the run rules' failures, the plate's diagnostics, the design rule check, the dialect's check and `availability.run`.
- **Diagnostics** are the tool, operation and run rules' failures ([above](#diagnostics)).
- **Design rule results** are the move and program rules' failures (below).
- **Importing** asks about the program rules' failures that offer a fix and makes the chosen ones (`resolveProgram`, `src/domain/design-rules/program-rules.ts`), as a program rule's quick fix does.
- **The height-map review** lists the height-map rules' failures in list order, and `height-map/flat` for a surface within the tolerance ([height-map.md](height-map.md#reviewing-the-height-map)).
- **The console's command line** holds a typed line to the connected machine's program rules the project reports as errors.
- **Diagnosis** shows each failure as a finding on the Device page and the Job tab, named by its rule or by the alarm it finds, with the rule's fixes as actions: a machine command, Reset, Stop, or reading the machine's switches, after which the rules are tested again ([device-controls.md](device-controls.md#diagnosis)).

A project sets how its configurable rules are reported, and their limits (`ruleSettings`, `RuleSettings`): a map by rule id of `{ severity, limit }` that holds only what differs from each rule's default (`savedRuleSettings`), so a rule it does not set follows its default. `RuleSettingsSchema` (`src/domain/rules/settings.ts`) refuses a setting for a rule that is not configurable and a limit outside its rule's range; a setting for an id this version does not know is kept as it is, and neither tested nor shown. `ruleSettings.set` changes them; they are saved with the project, kept across reloads and count as unsaved changes.

## Design rules

The design rules ([design-rules.md](design-rules.md)) are the rules a project sets: the move rules (`MOVE_RULES`, `src/domain/design-rules/move-rules.ts`: max cutting feed, plunge rate, cut depth and depth under the stock, the spindle stopped while cutting, rapid moves into the stock) and the program rules, which find what a machine would not run as written in NC, or should not run from a program, and offer fixes that keep its line numbers: those for every machine (`PROGRAM_RULES`, `common-rules.ts`) and a machine's own, in its kit's folder (the Z1's `Z1_RULES`, `src/domain/fixtures/makera-z1/program-rules.ts`). `checkDesignRules` (`check.ts`) runs the move rules on the compiled program's moves, measured over the stock's box widened by each tool's radius (without stock, below Z0 over the extent of its cuts), and the program rules of the plate's machine on each operation's own NC, from what the operations before it leave set. Its violations are `Diagnostic`s (`design-rule/<rule id>`) per rule and operation, with the program lines they concern, where the worst move ends, and for an NC file's program rule a `resolve-rule` fix. They are not part of the plate's diagnostics: the Job tab's run checklist blocks Run on an error, and nothing blocks export. Importing asks about the program rules' failures (`src/app/workspace/import-plan.ts`).

## Commands

`applyCommand(state, command)` is the only writer: it returns the next state or a reason. Commands cover plates (add, remove, select, move, rename, setup, move a setup item, dismiss a notice), fixtures (add, remove, update, lock, set the bed, use the device's defaults, take an edited definition), operations (add, remove, move, rename, pause before, source with an expected revision, its tool assignments), the tool table (assign, renumber), groups, the libraries, saving the project (its file name), height maps, the rule settings, anchor synchronisation, replacing the whole workspace (opening a project) and batches that apply several commands atomically. `WorkspaceStore` wraps it in a TanStack Store.

## Undo and redo

`WorkspaceStore` records the user's edits in a `History` (`src/app/workspace/history.ts`) for **Edit › Undo** and **Redo**: each step is the workspace before and after an edit, which share everything the edit left alone. Every command is an edit except the selection (`plate.select`), what the device reports (`anchors.sync`, `heightMap.store`), saving (`project.saved`) and dismissing a notice (`plate.dismissNotice`); a batch is an edit when it holds one, and the libraries restored from storage are dispatched with `record: false`. Those other changes outlast undo and redo: restoring a step's workspace replays the ones made after it (one that no longer applies changes nothing), and the selected plate stays selected while the restored workspace has it.

- An edit that leaves everything but the selection as it was is no step, and the workspace keeps its parts.
- Edits of one value merge into one step while each comes within a second of the one before: a plate's or an operation's name, a setup field (by the fields it sets), a fixture's placement (by the fields it sets) and its lock, the plate's bed, an operation's source and tools (as the PCB editor saves them), a plate's groups, the rule settings and each library. Any other edit, an undo or a redo starts a new step.
- A new edit drops the steps undone, and the history keeps the last 200. `workspace.replace` (opening or starting a project, a reload restoring its workspace) starts a new history.
- A restored workspace keeps the identity of its parts, and a change replayed on the workspace it was first made on gives the same result, so a project undone back to how it was saved has no unsaved changes (`hasUnsavedChanges` compares by identity).
- A removal's or a move's notification, and the tool library's Undo delete, undo their own step only while it is the latest (`WorkspaceStore.undo(step)`); after later edits they say so.

The Device tab's fixture library (`FixtureLibraryStore`) has a history of its own: edits of a bed setup's definitions (typing a fixture's name merges by field), name and anchors, adding and removing bed setups, the default one and the bed offset are steps; the selected profile and what a device reports are not, and the library restored from storage starts a new history. Plates set up for a device follow the anchors an undo or a redo restores, as they follow a bed offset.

Undo and Redo reach the history of the section on show: the workspace's on Prepare and Job, the fixture library's on Device. With a text field focused they undo its typing instead; the developer tools undo their own typing, which the main process asks of them; and while a dialog is open only typing is undone.

## Storage and files

- **No stored project**: every launch starts with a new project on the stored tool and stock libraries (`src/persistence/library-document.ts`), which the app keeps on its own; library edits are not project changes. Closing the window with unsaved project changes asks to save them first ([projects](step-nc-projects.md)). Reloading it (⌘R, or the dev server after a code change) keeps the workspace and its unsaved changes, in memory only ([architecture](architecture.md)). Projects and exported NC this version cannot read, from a newer version or in a format before version 4, are refused with a message.
- **Exported NC** carries the plate as a leading comment block (the plate envelope, [plate-definition.md](plate-definition.md)); an export whose NC was edited afterwards imports as a single program with a notice.
- **Projects** are STEP-NC files holding the workspace, PCB source files and recipes, and fixture models ([step-nc-projects.md](step-nc-projects.md)).
- **Tool library files** (`exportToolLibrary` and `importToolLibrary`, `src/formats/tool-library/library-file.ts`): an export is a zip of `tool-library.json`, the native library (`{"format": "openspindle-tool-library", "version": 2, "units": "mm", "tools": […]}`), and each photo and 3D model its tools keep, stored once (`images/1.webp`, `models/1.glb`): a tool names the file by its path relative to the JSON instead of holding a data URL, and bundled pictures and models keep their `/images/…` and `/models/…` paths. An import reads these archives, the JSON of earlier versions, and Fusion 360's JSON, `.tools` and ZIP libraries. It turns each named file back into a data URL before the tool is read like any other (upgraded, then checked), refuses a named file the archive lacks, and checks every 3D model as a chosen one is (`validateGlb`: one self-contained GLB). The limits are what memory allows rather than what 10,000 tools could hold (a 300 KB photo and a 1 MB model each, 13 GB): a 256 MB file, 160 MB of JSON (16 KB a tool) and 384 MB unpacked, which as data URLs (a third larger) is the 512 MB the app stores a library in. Reading an archive (`src/lib/zip.ts`) streams it: the central directory is read with at most 65,535 entries, handling ZIP64 or refusing it clearly, and each entry is inflated a few kilobytes at a time against these limits and the photo's or model's own, stopping as soon as its real decompressed bytes would pass one — whatever the entry's own stated size claims — and its length and CRC-32 are checked before it is used; only stored and deflated entries are read, and any other method is refused. An import that would take the library past 10,000 tools is refused, and so is an export an import would refuse.

# Guided workshop

Guided setup, facing and reusable processes open in a resizable sidebar beside the workspace, keeping the 3D view and machine controls accessible.

The Prepare toolbar offers Guided setup, Facing wizard and Reusable processes. Creating, editing, calculating, loading a process and previewing stock never command the machine. Run stays on Job. Physical Z1 connections enable the ordinary screen controls, jobs and probing adapter. Gamepad Step mode supports physical Z1 connections; Direct steering is simulator-only. Physical facing and controller behavior still need hands-on verification.

## Guided setup

Move between Stock, Workholding, Work zero, Tools and Review. These steps use the existing setup controls; edits immediately change the project and can be undone. Closing the guide keeps them. Stock placement and work zero are in bed millimetres. Work zero must match the CAM origin; choosing it in the model does not probe physical work Z. Plates with a touch-off operation keep work Z on stock top, so the guide locks that field and disables bottom-origin choices. Modeled fixtures do not confirm actual clamping.

## Facing and cutting data

Create a facing operation on a plate or select an existing Facing operation and choose **Edit facing and cutting data** in its inspector. Rectangles and starting top Z use work coordinates. Removal is below that top; clearance is above it. The raster starts beyond the rectangle along X by the cutter radius plus 1 mm, reverses between rows outside the rectangle, and retracts between depth passes. Leave that entry space clear. Stepover is limited to half the diameter, with at most 20,000 cutting rows across all depth passes.

**Facing pattern** selects Horizontal (X), Vertical (Y), or **Cross-hatch (X then Y)**. Both cross-hatch rasters run at the same Z for each depth layer: removal is not doubled. Existing operations without a pattern retain their horizontal path. Outside-stock entry needs cutter-radius-plus-1-mm space along the selected raster direction, along both X and Y for cross-hatch; the travel bounds check includes both directions. **Border first** is available with a verified cleared-pocket entry: each depth layer follows the free perimeter segments, routes around padded keep-outs, then cuts the selected raster(s), all at the same depth and cutting feed. A border or raster that cannot be reached safely blocks generation. Pocket cross-hatch stays connected between directions rather than plunging into material again. Pattern and border choices persist in saved projects, plate exports and reusable processes. Smaller stepover can reduce pass marks but does not correct spindle misalignment. The 20,000-row limit counts both raster directions.

**Entry method → Cleared pocket** uses X/Y and width/depth as a **cutter-centre rectangle**, without outside-X travel. Enter the pocket centre in work coordinates and its measured clear opening and depth from the starting top. The opening must be at least the cutter diameter plus 2 mm, and its clear depth at least total removal plus 0.1 mm. Confirm the actual opening, screws, pins and underlying metal; modeled dimensions alone never enable this entry. Each depth pass travels to the pocket at clearance, lowers in the clear opening, then follows a connected raster at cutting depth. Adjacent rows alternate direction and link at cutting feed; links route around the padded equipment footprints within the rectangle. An unreachable span blocks the operation rather than crossing a keep-out or silently skipping it. The cutter retracts after the depth pass, returning to the pocket only for the next depth pass. Links can cut previously uncut material, so review their engagement and feed as well as the rows. The entry confirmation is tied to the device, anchor frame and work origin; changes require verification again. Editing entry measurements, starting top or cutter diameter clears confirmation.

The Z1's **Modeled MDF recess** selector offers candidate screw recesses from the supplied `MDF+Bed+Replacement.step`: a 206 × 206 × 6 mm board, Ø10 mm counterbores 2 mm deep, above Ø5.4 mm through bores. Its hole pattern matches the bundled bed; STEP XY is translated by (-132, +88) and Z by -26 mm into the kit's bed frame. Candidate labels use bed XY; selection converts them to the current work XY and clears confirmation. Replace the dimensions with actual clearance measurements. **Use modeled XY travel as cutter-centre rectangle** fills the kit's 200 × 200 mm centre travel, without changing top Z, feeds, entry or stock. A 6 mm cutter can reach the modeled board's straight edges, but small corner patches and the protected setter patch remain unfaced. The CAD model does not establish the installed bed's registration, screw clearance or work Z.

Z1 Facing automatically excludes a square around the modeled tool setter opening at bed XY 181,181 (the bundled MDF model's 5.25 mm hole radius), enlarged by the cutter radius plus 2 mm clearance. The exclusion stays active when bed fixtures are hidden or disabled. With outside-stock entry, clipped row segments retract and re-enter from outside. With cleared-pocket entry, cutting links route around the exclusion and stay inside the cutter-centre rectangle. No cutting link crosses the protected patch, which stays unfaced; clearance travel stays above the modeled setter top. Preview, exported NC and reusable Facing processes regenerate the same exclusion from the current plate setup and cutter. Work XY must be anchored to locate it; Facing after origin probing is blocked because that measured origin would shift the keep-out. Face before origin probing, or remove the origin routine after independently confirming the work origin. The setter position and top are the kit's modeled values, not physical metrology or a fixture/holder collision check.

Choose a library flat end mill or straight flat-ended face mill with known diameter and cutting length. Rounded, tapered or differently sized tips are unsupported. Editing an unassigned Facing operation requires assigning its cutter in the plate's tool table; the assistant does not substitute another library tool. Changing the library diameter requires explicitly applying the new diameter in Facing. The kit checks its spindle range (Z1: 1,000–13,000 RPM), cutter-centre travel against the work envelope, cutting length, and cuts below the modeled stock bottom. Existing design rules provide further checks. These checks do not establish physical work Z or holder/clamp clearance.

No feed or RPM is guessed for a new operation. Load a named tool/material preset and explicitly apply it, or enter cutting data. Preset stepdown and stepover replace entered values only when their respective Use options are enabled; otherwise the current values stay. Chip load is calculated from feed / (RPM × cutting teeth). Once valid parameters are entered, a target chip load from your cutter data can calculate an alternative feed; use **Use calculated feed** to apply it. Save named cutting presets to the chosen tool. These are operator-entered or library values, not automatic material recommendations. Imported Fusion files retain their feeds and speeds.

Facing parameters are saved with the project and regenerate the program when opened. Job's source view shows the compiled program. Preview and review it before Run. Facing does not automatically shrink the stock model.

## Job checks and measurements

Each existing preflight row has **What this checks**, while failures keep their original fixes. All existing Run gates remain in place. A passing software checklist does not establish workholding, installed-tool dimensions or physical work Z.

After a completed inspection Run, completed feature reports offer **Use … in setup**. Choose a target and preview either the measured corner/center as work XY, or Boss center's opposing sides and spans as stock XY. A conversion requires the frozen Run's device-matched anchors from firmware, and an identical anchor snapshot on the target. Unknown or changed frames block application. Changed target data or a dismissed/replaced Run requires a fresh proposal. Simulation data needs an explicit acknowledgement. Applying changes project XY only; absolute Z, stock height and rotation remain entered values. The setup retains an audit of the last application, including Run, feature, source, anchors, applied XY and time; later edits may change the current values. Grid reports stay available for surface analysis and export, rather than being treated as absolute setup height.

## Reusable processes

Save a named snapshot of the selected plate: stock, placement, fixtures, work zero, ordered operations, pause-before choices and referenced tools. Processes persist locally across app launches in a separate versioned document. Loading requires matching device and anchor frame and creates a new plate with fresh identities. Rereading unchanged anchors is allowed even when the acquisition time changes; differing anchor coordinates, identities, source or bed placement still block loading. Changed library tools become separate copies; missing definitions stay unassigned. Compiled section selections are cleared when identities change. Custom fixture models remain references to the local Models library. Review actual dimensions, setup and cutting data for each use before Run. Saving errors and unreadable/newer documents use the app's existing recovery flow.

## Material removal

On Job, toggle **Material removal** above the viewer. The stock becomes a sampled 2.5D height field following the playback cursor, including partial moves; backward seeks rebuild it from uncut stock. With no playback cursor it shows the end of the program. Calculation runs in a worker; while it catches up, the display says **Updating sampled stock**. Off-plan live tool positions withhold the result. Toggling off restores the original stock box.

If calculation stops with an error, that reason stays visible when the timeline moves. Toggle removal off and on, or change the program, to begin a fresh calculation.

The model supports known straight flat-ended cutters and V-bits (chamfer mills, engraving bits and countersinks), including assigned implicit tools, positive rectangular stock and ordinary three-axis machining.

V-bits require a known cutting diameter, cutting length, tip diameter (0 for a sharp point), and taper or point angle in the tool library. Taper angle is measured from the axis; point angle is the full included angle, so a 90° V-bit uses a 45° taper. The preview sweeps the tapered profile and its flat tip along each XYZ move, limiting the cutting radius to the known diameter and cutting length. Missing or unsupported geometry is reported rather than approximated as a flat cutter. Facing still requires a flat-ended cutter.

Imported sources must pass the existing plain-NC capability reader with the kit’s grammar; unsupported cycles and absolute-center arcs are withheld even when the geometric parser can read their words. Arcs use the preview's tessellated chords. Generated probing operations may share a plate with cutting operations: their motion, routine moves and tool-setter touches remain on the playback timeline but never remove material or require a cutting-tool profile. Suppressed operations are ignored. The model shows nominal planned stock removal; it does not apply measured height-map compensation or certify a physically measured origin. Unsupported raw probing/compensation in imported NC, rotary axes, coordinate-changing machining operations, non-XY arcs, cutter compensation, unreadable lines and unavailable cutters are still reported as unsupported. **Settings → General → Material removal resolution** chooses 128, 256, 512 (default) or 1024 intervals along the longest stock side. The preference is saved on this computer and changing it rebuilds the active preview. For a 150 mm side, 128 gives about 1.17 mm spacing, 512 about 0.293 mm and 1024 about 0.146 mm. Sampling work and height/mesh memory grow roughly with the square of this setting. The selected resolution is never silently lowered. Programs estimated above 128 million sample visits are refused with a suggestion to lower the setting or preview a smaller operation set; at most 50,000 preview segments are accepted. The Job overlay displays the actual grid dimensions and spacing. Flat regions are merged into boundary-preserving quadtree fans; a dense ring remains beside cuts, and all non-flat regions retain the height samples. Geometry buffers are reused during playback. The height field remains dense, so this mesh optimization reduces rendered triangles rather than calculation or height-map memory. Narrow details can vanish, deep cuts clamp at stock bottom, and the model cannot show undercuts, tool/holder collisions or verify physical machining. It does not grant a preflight pass.

## Simulator trial

1. Connect the Z1 simulator, read its anchors and open Guided setup. Enter a small stock rectangle and check all five steps. Close/reopen to confirm edits persist; try Undo. On a plate with touch-off, verify work Z is locked to stock top and bottom-origin choices are unavailable.
2. Choose a known flat cutter with cutting length. Create Facing on stock well inside the bed so the extra X entry fits. Enter reviewed simulator-only RPM/feed/entry feed, shallow removal and clearance, or apply a complete library preset. Edit the operation afterward. Invalid or empty numeric fields must disable Apply; switching cutters or applying cutting presets must retain invalid geometry drafts; extra-tiny stepover must report the row budget. Change the library diameter and verify generation blocks until the new diameter is applied.
3. Check calculated chip load and explicitly calculate/apply a new feed. Save a named cutting preset and reopen the wizard to select it. Disable its Use stepdown and Use stepover options in the library, change the entered facing values, and apply that preset: the entered depth and width per pass must stay. Clear a Facing operation's tool assignment and reopen it: the missing cutter must be shown and Apply disabled. Save and reopen the project; confirm Facing remains editable with the same parameters. Imported G-code must retain its own feed values.
4. Open Job and inspect the explanations and fixes for its preflight rows. Play Facing without running the machine. Toggle Material removal: rotate the bed, pause partway, scrub forward and backward, and confirm the cut follows the cursor. After off-plan tracking, return quickly to an earlier playback point: no result ahead of that point should appear while the worker catches up. Toggle off to restore uncut stock. A plate with generated probing must show only the cutting operations removing stock; an unsupported cutting tool must still explain why removal is unavailable. Try an implicit-tool program and a later explicit tool change; widths must use the corresponding assigned cutters. A single imported file containing G81 or G90.1 must withhold removal with a reason.
5. Save the Facing plate as a named process. Reread unchanged simulator anchors and confirm loading remains available; changed anchor coordinates must still block it. Change one library tool, then load the process: it should create a new plate and separate tool copy, preserving the original library edit and operation order. Restart the app and verify the process still exists. Include a pending PCB recipe with a selected tool and preset: loading must preserve those selections on the restored tool copy. Review the loaded plate before Run.
6. With anchored work XY at bed 0,0 and a 6 mm flat cutter, preview a Facing rectangle at X150 Y150, width 30 and depth 35. Inspect the setter patch: later rows must stop short, with retracted travel rather than cutting links across it. Change cutter diameter and work origin, preserving the rectangle's bed placement; the protected patch must remain at the setter and expand with cutter radius. Hide the MDF fixture and confirm the exclusion remains. Inspect exported NC and a reloaded process for the same clipped path. Place origin probing before Facing and confirm Run/export block it; a Z touch-off alone may precede Facing.
7. Choose Cleared pocket and use the modeled XY travel rectangle. For a simulation-only trial, select the recess at bed X15 Y-7.5, use its nominal Ø10 × 2 mm dimensions with a 6 mm cutter and a shallow skim, and acknowledge the synthetic clearance. Preview and inspect one vertical entry at that pocket per depth pass, alternating row directions, lateral cutting-feed links, and routing around the setter exclusion without leaving the rectangle. A rectangle split into disconnected regions by a protected band must block generation. Clear a pocket field, then switch back to Outside stock: hidden pocket drafts must not prevent valid outside-entry parameters. Save/reopen a valid pocket operation; change the work origin or anchors and verify generation blocks until entry is checked again. A physically installed recess must be measured and located before its confirmation; the nominal simulator trial is not evidence of real clearance.

These trials require interaction with the desktop app and are pending user verification. Static checks and an independent code review are recorded separately.
